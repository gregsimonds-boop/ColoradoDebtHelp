Start with the right questions.

Credit cards, personal loans, and medical bills may raise different questions. Tell the provider what kind of debt you have and confirm whether its services are available in your state.

Debt settlement, credit counseling, and consolidation are different services. Ask exactly which approach is being proposed and how it would affect your finances.

Credit counseling

Consider nonprofit credit counseling for help reviewing your budget and potential debt-management options. Counseling and a repayment plan are not the same as debt settlement.

Debt settlement

A provider may attempt to negotiate with creditors. Creditors do not have to agree, and there is no guarantee that any or all debts will be settled.

Other approaches

Ask creditors directly about hardship arrangements. Compare any proposed consolidation loan carefully, including its interest rate, fees, and total repayment cost.

Important considerations

Clarity includes
the trade-offs.

Debt settlement can damage your credit. If payments stop, interest and late fees can increase what you owe, collection efforts may continue, and a creditor may sue. Creditors can refuse to settle.

Forgiven debt may have tax consequences. Review potential costs with the provider and consider independent financial, legal, or tax guidance before making a decision.

Before you call

A few helpful answers.

Is Debt Relief America a debt-settlement company?

No. This is an independent information and referral resource. Services are provided by independent companies, subject to availability and eligibility.

Is using this website free?

Our referral resource is free. Independent providers may charge fees for their services. Ask for all costs in writing before agreeing to any program.

Will my debt be reduced or my application approved?

No outcome is guaranteed. Eligibility, potential results, and provider availability depend on your circumstances, state, debt type, and other factors.

Will this affect my credit?

Debt settlement can negatively affect your credit, and missed payments can lead to additional costs and collection activity. Ask the provider about the specific risks of its proposed approach.

Is this a government debt-relief program?

No. Debt Relief America is not a government agency and is not affiliated with a government debt-forgiveness program.

What should I share on a first call?

Start with general information such as your state and approximate debt balances. Confirm the recipient’s identity and privacy practices before sharing account numbers, Social Security numbers, or payment information.
